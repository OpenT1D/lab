# 0026 — Conditional joint sensitivity and synthetic recovery

Status: accepted on 22 September 2026 for the requested next OT-026 step, starting from `b0bd514` in the existing isolated worktree.

The [BC-011 protocol](../research/m4-joint-identifiability-protocol.md) compares dynamic glucose, glucose plus insulin, and those channels plus C-peptide under known versus unknown clearance. Use the fixed-hepatic BC-008 hybrid and the BC-010 C-peptide equations. Vary only evoked secretion gain, peripheral insulin removal m4 and central C-peptide removal k3. The source's beta/K ratio, all other native physiology, C-peptide exchange rates and volume, input and appearance ratio remain known. This deliberately favorable conditional question can expose information gaps; it does not identify the complete biological model.

Preserve exact nominal basal glucose and plasma insulin, the SC depots and prescribed SC input in every observation arm. Recompute liver insulin and basal secretion from the steady insulin balances at each candidate m4, then initialize the source and C-peptide compartments at their corresponding equilibria. Otherwise an inconsistent initial inventory would introduce a startup transient into the inferred clearance signal. Basal secretion is a derived constraint, not an independently fitted capacity. In particular, “glucose only” retains basal insulin knowledge and refers to the dynamic observation channels. Gain scales evoked beta/K responsivity, not basal output, cell count or a treatment mechanism.

Repeated parameter fitting warrants a new monolithic execution wrapper: append passive C-peptide inventories and ledgers to the unchanged BC-008 derivative, driven by pancreatic Spo. This avoids interpolation within the inverse calculation and uses the existing pinned native runtime, which already supplies the required numerical libraries. No historical model, lock or result changes. BC-010 retains its separate-runtime, archived-input design. A monolithic passive branch can alter adaptive solver steps despite having no biological feedback; explicitly compare G/I with the original parent and check the C-peptide calculation against an independent matrix exponential, alongside solver, equilibrium and mass-balance checks.

Use the same synthetic observation realizations across nested sampling schedules and observation sets. Freeze noise scales, seeds, parameter bounds, starts and numerical gates before fitting. BC-011/2 amends the optimizer specification before any fit: use an explicit absolute log-coordinate difference step, with a bounded three-point formula near parameter bounds. A relative step in log coordinates can shrink near log(1)=0 and make numerical integration error dominate the derivative. Divide each residual by its fixed channel SD before squaring. These scales and bounds are engineering choices, not population ranges or assay specifications. Keep every attempted fit, bound hit and failure. In the unknown-clearance arm, k3 cannot affect glucose or insulin: label it unobservable for those channels and do not report an arbitrary optimizer value as an estimate.

Interpret the weighted log-parameter Jacobian and bounded multistart recovery as local numerical diagnostics. Full local rank or recovery from two starts does not prove global uniqueness, practical identifiability or reliable uncertainty intervals. The [Raue et al. method](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btp358) distinguishes those questions; BC-011 does not implement its profile likelihood. Independently check the gain/beta/K scaling redundancy, which the selected parameterization removes. Two noise realizations expose examples of noise sensitivity without estimating coverage or a recovery success rate.

The result will guide a later measurement-feasibility study, conditional on these assumptions. Uncertain baseline knowledge, hepatic extraction, insulin action, meal appearance, C-peptide kinetics and assay behavior still require separate assessment. External domain review and authorized human observations remain open; this step neither completes M4 nor establishes a clinical measurement schedule or therapy effect.
