# 0027 — C-peptide volume knowledge in joint synthetic inference

Status: accepted on 22 September 2026 for the next bounded OT-026 experiment, starting from `959d1d1` in the existing isolated worktree.

BC-011 found improved recovery when synthetic C-peptide observations supplemented glucose and insulin, conditional on fixed C-peptide volume and other physiology. The [BC-012 protocol](../research/m4-volume-sensitivity-protocol.md) tests this particular condition before expanding the dynamical model. Compare the same observations with correct, low, high and estimated central C-peptide volume, under known or estimated insulin and C-peptide removal rates. A single matched glucose-plus-insulin control for each rate, sampling and noise setting supplies the comparison without duplicating fits across a volume parameter absent from those observations.

Use the unchanged BC-011 forward model and its separate locked native environment. The additional multiplier enters only the observation equation `Cp=Q1/(0.0655*volume_scale)`, with Q1 in pmol/kg, volume in L/kg and concentration in pmol/L. Preserve the amount states, pancreatic input, equilibrium initialization, elimination ledgers and glucose/insulin feedback. In particular, do not multiply initial C-peptide amounts by volume to preserve an observed baseline. The [Polonsky model card](../research/models/polonsky1986-cpeptide.md) records the original compartment definitions and nominal central volume. Its source does not establish a usable empirical uncertainty distribution; fixed multipliers 0.8/1.2 and fitting bounds 0.5–1.5 are declared engineering choices.

Call the inference regimes **known rates** and **estimated rates**, because an unknown volume means that the kinetics are not all known. C-peptide dimensional clearance is `k3*V` in L/(kg min), so fixing the first-order removal rate k3 while changing V does not fix that clearance. Treating the volume as an independent nuisance parameter examines a model assumption; it is not a population model of volume/rate covariance. Keep exchange rates, equimolar appearance, hepatic structure, insulin action, meal kinetics and exact basal glucose/insulin conditioning fixed and visible.

All volume arms use the same BC-011 truth at volume one, the same shared observation realizations, channel SDs, nested schedules and original parameter starts. Append two prespecified volume starts. Changing an assumed volume never changes the generating data or rescales the noise. Reuse bounded log-coordinate fitting with fixed absolute differences, preserve every attempted start and retain optimizer or model failures. Apply the noiseless recovery criterion to correct- and estimated-volume fits and observable GI parameters. A completed fixed-wrong-volume fit can be biased by design; its residuals and parameter errors are findings rather than failed truth-recovery criteria.

Verify exact parent equivalence at volume one, identical amount states and G/I at every tested volume, inverse concentration scaling and the independent derivative `dCp/dlog(V)=-Cp`. The fasting observation constrains `Sb(m4)/(k3*V)`; k3 and volume therefore have equal log sensitivities at that one equilibrium measurement. Their dynamic effects need not be identical, because k3 changes time constants and volume only changes concentration scale. Likewise, the earlier free-appearance/volume scaling ambiguity is not automatically a gain/volume symmetry in a model where gain changes glucose feedback and does not scale basal secretion. Keep free appearance scaling outside this experiment.

Report weighted local sensitivity with difference-step and solver refinement at each declared volume anchor, alongside parameter recovery, channel residuals, boundaries and start disagreement. Full local rank is not proof of global uniqueness or stable inference from noisy observations. Two noise realizations do not establish uncertainty coverage or a clinical sampling schedule. An additional free volume parameter may lower residuals without improving biological knowledge. Preserve historical BC-011 artifacts and numerical code, and reconstruct the new results from their own source and protocol snapshots.

This experiment refines the need for independent kinetic information within the existing synthetic model. It neither establishes human identifiability nor completes OT-026 or M4. Unknown exchange rates, uncertain basal measurements, population calibration and external biological assessment remain open. No human data access, outreach, treatment mechanism or cell-count interpretation is part of this decision.
