# 0072 — Bound source publication rights and native runtime exposure

Date: 25 October 2026

Status: accepted for local source preparation; platform activation pending.

## Context

The maintainer confirmed authority to license original OpenT1D material under
Apache-2.0, and confirmed ownership and receipt at the chosen confidential
contact. Two historical execution receipts embed complete upstream native
parameter rows with unresolved separate provenance. Frozen native locks also
retain known advisories; silently upgrading them would change the identities
bound by numerical protocols.

## Decision

Offer original source, synthetic fixtures and attributed research records under
their applicable terms. Include verified pinned upstream notices and the DejaVu
license for glyph outlines embedded in generated figures. Record the exact
source-only rights boundary in `publication-rights.json`. Do not distribute
private observations, article originals, weights or bundled native runtimes.

Withhold the two complete parameter rows from public historical receipt copies,
record their file/fragment hashes and preserve originals privately. No replacement
parameters, result changes or historical hash matches are invented. This extends
the explicit redaction policy in [0071](0071-public-source-history.md).

Keep historical native locks intact. In the independent public snapshot,
task-directed installation refuses projects outside a reviewed allowlist before
launching an installer. Root tooling is patched for its current advisories.
The supported initial exercise is the two synthetic standard-library MMTT tasks.
Default CI checks that source scope; historical native campaigns require an
explicit manual request in the private, non-snapshot archive. A patched native
successor needs its own dependency, numerical, process, rights and reader checks.

## Consequences

[Licensing](../licensing.md), [publication security](../publication-security.md)
and the [source map](../../publication-source-map.json) expose the actual limits.
Automatic installation controls are not a hostile-code sandbox. Historical
alerts remain visible; excluding their runtimes is a mitigation, not a fix or
dismissal. The public source branch policy requires the check that actually runs.
The actual public destination and server controls remain separate activation
steps. No supported release, empirical result or independent acceptance follows.
