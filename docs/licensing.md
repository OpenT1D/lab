## Third-party material

Before distributing an upstream asset, record its source, exact version, copyright holder where known, license or access terms, required notices and any modifications. Keep the relevant notices with the asset and include them in distribution artifacts.

Dependencies installed by repository tooling retain their own licenses. The repository contains implemented adapters and equation implementations as well as research reports. Pinned engines and optional methods are installed separately; their license declarations do not establish rights for every upstream parameter table or dataset. The [publication inventory](research/publication-preparation.md#assets-and-rights) identifies the actual source-checkout boundary. A bundled-runtime distribution needs its own complete dependency and notice inventory.

An article, its equations, implementation and associated parameter or patient files can have different terms. Verify each asset actually used. Citation and license compliance are separate requirements: credit alone does not grant reuse rights.

## Optional integrations

Evaluate distribution obligations for the actual combination of components. An optional installation, adapter or separate process does not automatically settle compatibility. Document the chosen integration and distribution method before distributing an engine with different requirements.

For GPL components, consult the applicable license and the upstream terms; the [GNU GPL FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#MereAggregation) explains relevant distinctions about aggregation and combined programs. Where compatibility remains unresolved, keep that asset out of the distribution while the question is resolved.

## Contributions and future releases

Contributions intentionally submitted for inclusion follow the [contribution policy](../CONTRIBUTING.md#rights-data-and-attribution). There is currently no separate CLA or copyright assignment.

Before a release, check the files actually included, not just the dependency declarations. Preserve required notices, include license texts, describe restricted optional components and verify that examples and generated outputs can be published. Add asset-specific notices when assets are introduced; do not maintain an invented inventory of future dependencies.
